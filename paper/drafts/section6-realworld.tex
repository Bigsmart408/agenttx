Each non-clean run begins with a scripted recovery trajectory inside the
benchmark workspace. A faulty producer and dependent artifact are interleaved
with independent notes, followed by an injected-fault guard.
This guard detects the seeded fault before the original task begins.
Each policy determines the retained state, lost notes are replayed when needed,
and the live model then attempts the original task.
Clean starts without this trajectory.

Checkpoints receive the registered pre-fault boundary.
Causal ranks candidates through failed-step dependencies and checks them with
an injection-specific marker verifier.

\textbf{Workloads and success.}
We evaluated all 300 SWE-Bench Lite tasks~\cite{swebench} with SWE-agent and
five recovery policies, using GPT-5.6 Luna.
Each task--policy cell has one attempt; 1,499 of 1,500 cells have complete
results.
The clean-reference timeout incurs 1,958,229 known tokens; final in-flight
usage is unknown.

Success requires the target \texttt{FAIL\_TO\_PASS} tests and applicable
recovery-state, completion, and commit checks.
We report target-test recovery success. Fault injection selects source paths
from gold-patch file metadata, and the compact recovery handoff can include
these paths.

Terminal-Bench~\cite{tbench} covers all 241 tasks with SWE-agent and Codex under the same
five policies and model.
Its 2,410 unique task--harness--policy cells contain 3,121 recorded original,
retry, and repair attempts.
We report each cell's latest attempted outcome after one authorized ordinary
failure retry and separately audited environment/provider repairs.
The final model-session and verifier limits are 3,600\,s and 1,800\,s;
historical configuration revisions remain separate in cost pairing.
Full recovery success requires official tests and the recovery-state/context
checks; 2,154 cells pass this contract, versus 2,156 official verifier passes.

\textbf{Comparison and accounting.}
AgentTX and the clean reference use compact context;
no rollback, temporal checkpoints, and whole-branch rollback use full context.
Tokens use API-reported usage and include main execution and replay.
For TB, each cell's cost sums all recorded original, failed, retry, and repair
attempts, including attempts preceding the latest outcome.
We retain all known spend, totaling 4.804 billion TB tokens; 235 historical
attempts have incomplete usage, which we leave unknown.

Cost pairs contain tasks whose two latest outcomes satisfy the full
contract and whose cells have complete accounting.
TB pairs share a compatible configuration across their entire histories;
mixed configurations and documented environment exclusions are recorded
separately.
We aggregate disjoint pairs across compatible strata and report
$1-\sum T_{\mathrm{AgentTX}}/\sum T_{\mathrm{control}}$.
The 95\% intervals use 20,000 paired task bootstrap samples, resampling within
observed configuration strata for TB.

\textbf{Task outcomes.}
Figure~\ref{fig:sweagent-full} and Table~\ref{tab:swe-full-results} show that
success differences depend on workload and harness.
On SWE, AgentTX succeeds on 285 tasks, compared with 282 for no rollback,
284 for checkpoints, and 243 for whole-branch rollback.
On TB/SWE-agent, AgentTX and whole-branch rollback both succeed on 208 tasks;
the other policies succeed on 203--206.
On TB/Codex, AgentTX succeeds on 223 tasks, below whole-branch rollback's 227
and the clean reference's 225.

\textbf{Model cost.}
On SWE's jointly successful pairs, AgentTX reduces cumulative tokens by
58.43\%, 17.55\%, and 27.86\% against no rollback, checkpoints, and
whole-branch rollback, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:swe-paired-savings}).
The corresponding TB reductions are 24.70\%, 44.69\%, and 35.91\% with
SWE-agent, and 26.47\%, 25.39\%, and 28.43\% with Codex.
All three operational-control comparisons have positive lower confidence
bounds in each cohort.

For scale, the 176 TB/SWE-agent pairs against no rollback consume 41.38
million AgentTX tokens versus 54.95 million control tokens.
The corresponding 200 Codex pairs consume 425.41 million versus 578.58
million tokens, including their full attempt histories.
Across complete SWE runs, including failures, AgentTX averages 135,004 tokens
versus 311,673 for no rollback.

\textbf{Per-task variation.}
Figure~\ref{fig:realworld-ratios} separates tasks with lower and higher
token costs. Against temporal checkpoints, AgentTX uses fewer tokens on
188/278 SWE tasks (67.6\%), 100/175 TB/SWE-agent tasks (57.1\%), and
138/197 TB/Codex tasks (70.1\%). It uses more tokens on the remaining
32.4\%, 42.9\%, and 29.9\%, respectively.
On TB/Codex machine-learning/training tasks,
AgentTX costs 10.54\% more than no rollback (19 pairs) and 6.48\% more than
checkpoints (20 pairs).
